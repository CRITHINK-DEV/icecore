# Spike S2: payload limits

Story 144aefb3 (S12). Measured on 31 Oct 2026 in the sandbox, with dbt-core 1.8.10 and dbt-snowflake 1.8.4.

- Raw measurements are in `spikes/results/s2.json`.
- The probes are in `integration_tests/macros/spikes/s2_payload.sql`, which reuses the S1 macros.
- The tests are in `integration_tests/tests/spikes/test_s2_payload.py`.

Final sandbox run: **55 passed in 2752.77 s**. Every case asserts the measured behaviour, and absence is asserted with
`assert_no_events` over 180 s. `s2.json` holds 55 cases, each with a `call_ok` key.

## Framing: the Gate 1 write path

Gate 1 chose option B: two persisted procedures that hooks `CALL` through `run_query`, guarded (S1.md).

- **`ICECORE_EMIT(events ARRAY)`** (SQL) writes one SPAN_EVENT per event through `SYSTEM$ADD_EVENT`, inside the procedure's single
  span.
- **`ICECORE_EMIT_RUN(spans ARRAY)`** (Python) writes one custom span per element, with explicit times and attributes.

So the limits that matter are:

- the size of **one value** (an attribute) through each procedure;
- the size and count of **one batch** (one CALL).

The probes below measure both. The S1 temporary-procedure mechanisms (`sql_log`, `sql_event`, `python_log`, `python_attr`) are
kept for comparison.

## Single records: one record per CALL

`s2_payload(run_id, mechanism, size_kb)` sends **one CALL that emits one record**. That record's message, or its `icecore.payload`
attribute, is `size_kb × 1024` ASCII `x` characters.

| Mechanism | Payload in | Path |
|---|---|---|
| `proc_event` | SPAN_EVENT attribute | **ICECORE_EMIT** probe (persisted, guarded CALL) |
| `sql_event` | SPAN_EVENT attribute (`SYSTEM$ADD_EVENT`) | S1 SQL temporary procedure |
| `sql_log` | LOG message (`SYSTEM$LOG_INFO`) | S1 SQL temporary procedure |
| `python_attr` | Python LOG attribute (`extra=`) | S1 Python temporary procedure |
| `python_log` | Python LOG message | S1 Python temporary procedure |

**Every call succeeded** (`call_ok`, 55 of 55, batches included), and no ICECORE_EMIT call raised an icecore warning.

| Size | proc_event (ICECORE_EMIT) | sql_event | sql_log | python_attr | python_log |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8, 64, 256 KB | intact | intact | intact | intact | intact |
| 384 KB | intact | intact | intact | intact | intact |
| 512 KB | intact (524,288) | intact | intact | intact | **truncated: 524,252 + `...`** |
| 768 KB | intact | intact | intact | intact | **truncated** |
| 960 KB | intact (983,040) | intact | intact | intact | **truncated** |
| 1024 KB | **dropped**: no row in 180 s | **dropped** | **dropped** | **dropped** | **truncated** |

What the table shows:

- **Per-record limit: between 960 KB and 1024 KB.**
  - Above it, the record is **silently dropped**: the CALL succeeds and returns `ok`, no error appears, and no row lands.
  - This applies to SPAN_EVENT attributes, SQL log messages and Python attributes alike.
  - Whether the procedure's own SPAN lands with a dropped single record is not shown: the test reads that span once, right after
    the CALL, with no wait (`proc_event.1024.span_landed = 0`). For the oversize batches below, read after the 180 s absence
    check, it did land (`proc_batch.16x64.span`, `32x64.span`, `64x64.span`: `span_landed = 1`).
- **Python log messages are truncated at 524,252 characters plus `...`** (`landed_len` 524,255).
  - Truncation applies from 512 KB up.
  - No attribute flags it: the record's attribute keys stay `code.*`, `icecore.payload_kb` and `icecore.run_id`.
  - SQL log messages are not truncated below the drop limit.
- **dbt wall time per single-record CALL** (`dbt_wall_s`):
  - SQL temporary procedures (`sql_event`, `sql_log`, every size): 1.55–2.49 s.
  - Python temporary procedures (`python_attr`, `python_log`, every size): 3.80–4.63 s.
  - ICECORE_EMIT: 1.78 s at 8 KB (`proc_event.8`), rising to 10.13 s at 960 KB and 11.26 s at 1024 KB. The time is spent building and sending the
    literal, not on the server.

## Batches: many records in one CALL

### ICECORE_EMIT: n span events in one CALL (`test_s2_proc_batch_size`, macro `s2_proc_batch`)

| Batch | Total | Landed | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 × 64 KB | 512 KB | **8/8**, intact | seq 1–8 |
| 12 × 64 KB | 768 KB | **12/12** | |
| 15 × 64 KB | 960 KB | **15/15** | |
| 16 × 64 KB | 1 MB | **0/16** (asserted absent for 180 s) | the procedure's SPAN lands, with no `dropped_events_count` |
| 32 × 64 KB | 2 MB | **0/32** | same |
| 64 × 64 KB | 4 MB | **0/64** | same; 149.7 s dbt wall |
| 128 × 1 KB | 128 KB | **128/128** | seq 1–128 |
| 129 × 1 KB | 129 KB | **128/129** | **seq 2–129 kept**; span `dropped_events_count = 1` |
| 256 × 1 KB | 256 KB | **128/256** | seq 129–256 kept; `dropped_events_count = 128` |
| 1000 × 1 KB | 1000 KB | **128/1000** | seq 873–1000 kept; `dropped_events_count = 872` |

Answers to the three questions:

- **(a) Largest total that lands in one CALL:** 960 KB (15 × 64 KB), within at most 128 events.
- **(b) Bytes or count: both, applied in this order.**
  - Count first: a span keeps at most **128 events**, the **last** 128, and the oldest are discarded.
  - Then bytes: if the kept events total about **1 MB** or more, **none** land.
- **(c) All or nothing?**
  - **By size, yes**: the whole batch is dropped, silently, and the span carries no drop count.
  - **By count, no**: the last 128 land, and the span's `RECORD:dropped_events_count` reports how many were dropped. That is the
    only trace of the loss, and it sits on a record the views may not read.

### ICECORE_EMIT_RUN: n spans in one CALL (`test_s2_emit_run_batch_size`, macro `s2_emit_run_batch`)

| Batch | Total | Landed | dbt wall s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 × 1 KB | 100 KB | **100/100**, intact | 4.91 |
| 200 × 1 KB | 200 KB | **200/200** | 5.30 |
| 15 × 64 KB | 960 KB | **15/15** | 12.94 |
| 16 × 64 KB | 1 MB | **16/16** | 14.00 |
| 64 × 64 KB | 4 MB | **64/64** | 153.81 |

Each custom span is its own record with its own (empty) event list. Neither the 128-event limit nor the roughly 1 MB batch limit
applies to an ICECORE_EMIT_RUN CALL. The per-record limit still applies to each span's attributes: below 1024 KB per span.

**Time grows steeply with batch size, and it is client-side.** For a 4 MB CALL, Snowflake reported about 3–4 s of execution
*(query history, exploration)*, yet the dbt run-operation took about 150 s. The difference is dbt and Jinja building, escaping and
logging the literal.

## Payload cap and chunk size for option B

- **Keep the spec default `payload_cap_kb = 64`** per value. It is far below every limit measured.
- **Hard ceiling for any override: 256 KB per record**, summed over all attributes. That leaves headroom under the roughly 1 MB
  record drop.
- **Flag truncation in icecore**, for example `icecore.truncated = true` and the original length. Snowflake drops oversize records
  silently, and Python log messages are truncated without a flag.
- **ICECORE_EMIT chunks: at most 100 events and 512 KB per CALL.** The measured limits are 128 events and less than 1 MB.
  - Past 128, the oldest events are lost: only the span's `dropped_events_count` shows it.
  - Past about 1 MB, the whole chunk is lost.
  - 512 KB also keeps dbt time near 4 s.
- **ICECORE_EMIT_RUN chunks: up to 512 KB per CALL**, for client-side time rather than for a Snowflake limit.
  - 200 spans and 4 MB landed whole, but 4 MB took 150 s of dbt time against 5 s for 200 KB.
  - Each span stays under the 256 KB per-record ceiling.
- **Put large values in attributes, not in log messages**, which Python truncates at 512 KiB.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] **Each mechanism and size: call success and landed length recorded in s2.json.**
  - Keys `<mechanism>.<size_kb>` cover 8, 64, 256 and 1024 KB, and `threshold.<mechanism>.<size_kb>` covers 384, 512, 768 and
    960 KB.
  - Each holds `call_ok`, `landed_rows`, `landed_len`, `payload_chars`, `intact`, `tail` and `run_id`.
  - Absent cases also carry `<key>.absent_after_s = 180`.
- [x] **Any size: the dbt call does not fail.** All 55 calls returned success, including dropped and truncated records and
  oversize batches.
- [x] **S2.md names the payload cap for the chosen encoding.** 64 KB by default, a 256 KB ceiling per record, ICECORE_EMIT chunks of
  at most 100 events and 512 KB, ICECORE_EMIT_RUN chunks up to 512 KB, and truncation flagged by icecore.

## Deviations from the Phase 1 plan (Task 4)

- Mechanisms: added `python_attr` and `proc_event` (ICECORE_EMIT) to the plan's `sql_log`, `sql_event` and `python_log`.
- The S1 mechanisms run through a temporary procedure, because S1 showed anonymous statements never reach the database's event
  table.
- Added threshold sizes 384, 512, 768 and 960 KB, plus two batch tests, `test_s2_proc_batch_size` and
  `test_s2_emit_run_batch_size`.
- Tests assert the measured outcome (intact, truncated or dropped) instead of failing on a drop, so the suite stays green.
